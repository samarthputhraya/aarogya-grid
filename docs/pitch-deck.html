02
Problem
The problem

A sub-centre runs out of anti-snake venom in monsoon. The vials exist.

They are ninety minutes away, in a facility that will write them off unused in six weeks. Nobody knows either fact.

Both facilities report into a paper register that reaches the district office weeks later, if at all. India's public health supply chain does not primarily fail on procurement volume — it fails on visibility, and on lateral movement: the ability to see a shortage forming and move stock sideways before it becomes a stock-out.

Solution
The forecasting problem nobody accounts for

A stock ledger records what was dispensed — not what was needed.

Once a facility hits zero, demand keeps arriving and stops being recorded. Fit naively on that ledger and you systematically under-forecast exactly the facilities that are already failing — then allocate them less, so they stock out again.

We fit with stocked-out periods excluded. Measured against the same estimator run on uncensored demand, so seasonality and recency cancel and only the censoring effect remains.

06
Finding
What the model found

Visibility is not the binding constraint. Unit economics is.

Of the needs the optimiser could not serve, we expected most to fail because the stock simply wasn't there. That is not what happens.

94.0% fail the benefit/cost gate. The medicine exists — only 1.5% of declined needs fail for want of donor stock anywhere. It sits a median 35.6 km away, and moving it costs more than the shortfall it averts because the model prices a separate vehicle trip per drug.

That reframes the intervention. Better dashboards do not fix this. Pooled district-level logistics does — one scheduled milk-run makes hundreds of individually uneconomic transfers economic at once.

We could have shown a ₹2 crore headline. It would have been a shadow price on averted shortfall doing 115% of the work.

Instead the multiplier is an explicit dial. The plan breaks even when one unit of averted unmet demand is valued at ₹6.11. Whether a dose of a Vital medicine reaching a patient is worth that is a ministry's judgement, not an engineer's — so we surface the parameter rather than bury it in a headline.

AI approach
Google AI — Gemini operates the grid

The officer asks in Hindi. The model calls the tools. Every number is quoted, never computed.

Live · District Health Officer asks
"Bastar mein kaun se centre par dawa khatam hone wali hai?"
1  list_positions {limit:10} → 10 rows
2  district_status {} → 1 row
3  drug_reference {Ceftriaxone} → 1 row
4  drug_reference {Artesunate} → 1 row
answered from 4 calls · 7 facilities and 3 drugs cited · 22.2s

Nine tools, all pure functions over already-computed state. The tool-call trace is shown in the interface — a grounded answer nobody can check is indistinguishable from a confident guess.

NO IDS
The model is never given a district code, facility id or drug id to emit. Asked about a district it had not been handed, it invented the code "Lucknow" — so arguments are natural-language names, resolved deterministically inside the tool.
NO MATHS
It quotes numbers, never derives them. Testing caught it turning a 0.998 probability into "99.8%" — correct arithmetic, but every ratio now ships a precomputed percent to quote instead.
HAND LOOP
The SDK's automatic function calling is deliberately unused: we reject unknown tool names, Zod-validate every argument before it touches data, and record the trace ourselves rather than asking the model what it did.
AUDITED
Every numeric token in the model's prose is matched against the payloads it actually saw. The audit is proven able to fail by tampering a known-good answer. Result: zero hallucinated numbers across 8 scenarios.

Provenance
What is real, and what is not

Stated plainly, because the honest answer is the stronger one.

Real

Districts, their coordinates and state assignments. Facility tier structure, bed norms and the sanctioned staffing establishment per IPHS 2022. A 47-item catalogue — 46 drugs from India's National List of Essential Medicines plus one tracked consumable — with VED classification, pack units, cold-chain flags and unit costs.

Simulated

The stock ledger, bed occupancy, staff attendance — and district catchment populations. All from a seeded, deterministic simulator parameterised from IPHS norms and published seasonality, not fitted to observed data. District and state rankings reflect a synthetic reliability parameter, not real performance.

Real PHC inventory data is not public. Rather than claim otherwise, we built the seam that makes the swap a one-file change — and every number in this deck is reproducible from a seed, by anyone, from the public repository.
